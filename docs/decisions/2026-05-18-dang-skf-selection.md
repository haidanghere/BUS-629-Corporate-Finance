## Executive Summary

This memo selects AB SKF (SKFB: Nasdaq Stockholm) as the subject company for the BUS 629 ratio analysis project. SKF is a Swedish industrial manufacturer of bearings, seals, and lubrication systems with FY2025 net sales of SEK 91,583M. The selection is grounded in direct professional familiarity, a five-year analytical arc shaped by a major Automotive divestiture (2023) and a 2025 global restructuring program, and confirmed access to five years of audited IFRS financials from Annual Reports 2021–2025.

## 2. Selection Rationale

**Career relevance.** I serve as Head of People Experience at SKF Vietnam, giving me direct exposure to the company's operational priorities, cost structure, and strategic agenda. This insider context sharpens ratio interpretation beyond what an external analyst can bring.

**Analytical interest.** The 2021–2025 window spans three distinct phases: a two-segment structure (Industrial + Automotive) through 2022; the Automotive divestiture to Magna International (announced June 2022, closed 2023), which fundamentally refocused the portfolio; and a 2025 global rightsizing program (MSEK –3,918 in items affecting comparability) that depressed reported operating margin to 8.5% while adjusted margin held at ~12.7%. The reported-vs-adjusted gap is a recurring analytical thread that makes SKF especially instructive for ratio analysis.

**International angle.** SKF generates revenue across 40+ currencies. FX translation alone reduced reported 2025 net sales by –6.6 percentage points — a concrete example of cross-border distortion directly relevant to this course.

## 4. Preliminary Observations

1. **I expect the 2023 Automotive divestiture to explain the majority of the margin improvement in post-2022 Industrial results** because the Automotive segment dragged group margins throughout 2021–2022 (Automotive: 6.3% in 2021, 2.4% in 2022 vs. Industrial: 15.9% and 11.3%). Isolating Industrial-only performance will test whether the structural improvement is durable.

2. **I expect net debt/EBITDA to show the most consistent improvement of any leverage metric across 2021–2025** because it compressed from 1.5x (2022 peak) to 1.0x (2025) alongside a net debt/equity decline from 38.3% to 21.6% — a deliberate, sustained deleveraging story backed by investment-grade ratings.

3. **I expect adjusted ROCE to hold near SKF's stated 14% target throughout the period while reported ROCE shows high volatility** because actual adjusted figures (14.9% → 12.6% → 15.4% → 14.2% → 14.3%) cluster near target, whereas reported ROCE swings from 9.6% to 14.8% depending on restructuring charge size. This gap is the clearest test of whether one-time items are truly non-recurring.

## 6. Data Collection Plan

**Statements:** Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow Statement, and Notes (segment data, FX disclosures, items affecting comparability).

**Period:** FY2021–FY2025. Years 2021–2022 include both Industrial and Automotive segments; Industrial-only segment data from those years will be used to ensure comparability with the post-divestiture 2023–2025 single-segment structure.

**Reported vs. adjusted:** Both tracked in parallel. IAC by year: 2021 (–81), 2022 (–1,672), 2023 (–1,893), 2024 (–1,844), 2025 (–3,918 MSEK).

**Currency:** Primary analysis in SEK; USD equivalents at year-end Riksbank spot rates where contextually useful.

**Standard:** IFRS throughout. Peer comparison with Schaeffler (IFRS) is clean; NSK (J-GAAP) will be flagged where reconciliation is needed.
