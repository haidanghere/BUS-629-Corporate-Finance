# AB SKF Peer Benchmarking — FY2024 Bearings Industry Comparison
**Stage 5 extra-credit deliverable · BUS-629 VEMBA · Hai Dang**

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This extra-credit benchmark places AB SKF inside the global bearings peer set — the five publicly-listed bearings manufacturers SKF itself identifies in the Annual Report 2025 ("the market is led by SKF, along with other major international players like Schaeffler, Timken, NSK, NTN and JTEKT") — to test whether SKF's FY2025 reading of negative EVA, compressed ROE (6.86%), and reported operating margin of 5.60% is a **company-specific underperformance** or a **sector-wide cyclical condition** that all bearings names share. *Sourcing note up front*: SKF figures throughout this document are verified against the Stage 3 Excel model and FY2025 Annual Report; peer figures (Schaeffler, NSK, NTN, JTEKT, Timken) are **approximate FY2024 reported values** drawn from publicly available company financial summaries and rounded to one decimal place. They are precise enough to anchor structural positioning and the analytical framework that follows, but the second decimal place should be read as approximate. The analytical value of this benchmark is in the *positioning and structural-gap finding*, not in the precision of any individual peer figure. The answer to the framing question above is **both, in different proportions**. On the cyclical dimension, four of the five peers (Schaeffler, NSK, NTN, JTEKT) print FY2024 ROE in the 2.5–5% range and operating margins in the 3–5% range — well below 10-year averages and squarely in the territory SKF occupies on reported figures. On the company-specific dimension, the single North American peer (Timken) prints operating margins above 15% and ROE above 14%, demonstrating that the cycle is not the binding constraint when business mix is right.

Three findings drive the report. **First**, SKF sits in the *middle of the peer set* on size (USD ~9B revenue, behind JTEKT's USD ~11.7B and Schaeffler's combined USD ~19.5B post-Vitesco, ahead of NSK and NTN) but ranks **fourth of six on operating margin** on reported FY2024 figures. **Second**, on the *adjusted* operating margin basis SKF publishes (12.3% FY2024, 12.7% FY2025), SKF moves up to **second** — above Schaeffler, NSK, NTN, JTEKT — but remains below Timken (15.9%) and short of SKF's own 14% long-term adjusted-operating-margin target. The reported-vs-adjusted bridge is therefore *not just an internal communication issue* — it is the difference between a 4th-of-6 and 2nd-of-6 peer-relative ranking. **Third**, SKF's balance-sheet metrics (total debt ratio 48%, current ratio 2.11x, TIE 5.64x) are *better than the European/Japanese cohort* and broadly in line with Timken, confirming that capital structure is not the operational gap. The strategic implication: SKF's path to peer-leading returns runs entirely through margin expansion, and the peer evidence supports the Stage 5 thesis that the planned 2026 Automotive separation plus the FY2025 rightsizing program is the right lever-pair.

## 2. Peer Universe Selection & Rationale

The peer set is **the exact competitor universe SKF itself identifies** in the Annual Report 2025: "the market is led by SKF, along with other major international players like Schaeffler, Timken, NSK, NTN and JTEKT. These top six manufacturers account for more than half of the global rolling bearing market." Anchoring to SKF's own competitive set avoids the analyst-discretion risk of cherry-picking favourable peers.

**Direct German peer: Schaeffler AG (SHA0.DE).** Schaeffler is SKF's closest structural analogue — both are family-influenced European bearings majors with industrial + automotive segments, both face the European electric-vehicle transition. Schaeffler completed the Vitesco merger (closed October 2024) creating an enlarged combined entity. SKF's own Automotive separation is planned for 2026 (per AR 2025), meaning during FY2024–25 the two firms occupied similar "transitioning bearings major" positions. The Schaeffler merger complicates FY2024 comparability — I use Schaeffler's reported combined entity figures and flag the timing effect in §9.

**Japanese cohort: NSK Ltd (6471.T), NTN Corporation (6472.T), and JTEKT Corporation (6473.T).** The three Japanese bearings firms operate on a March-ending fiscal year that I treat as "FY2024" when the period ends March 2024. NSK is the largest standalone Japanese bearings firm; NTN is a smaller but more bearings-pure competitor that has struggled with profitability and is currently in a multi-year restructuring; JTEKT carries significant steering-systems exposure (Toyota Group affiliation) which lowers its margin profile structurally and offers a useful "what if SKF had stayed in automotive bearings" counterfactual. The three Japanese firms together represent the *low-margin / restructuring-cohort* benchmark.

**North American leader: Timken Company (TKR).** Timken is the global benchmark for *integrated bearings + power transmission* margin discipline and operates at 4–5x SKF's reported operating margin on the FY2024 cycle. Timken alone anchors the upper bound of what mainstream bearings-industry profitability can look like with the right segment mix and earlier-completed rightsizing programs.

**What was excluded.** Minebea Mitsumi (precision miniature bearings) was excluded for product mix divergence (consumer electronics focus, not industrial). Regional Chinese firms (C&U Group, ZWZ) were excluded for disclosure-quality concerns at the consolidated level. RBC Bearings (US, aerospace specialist) was considered but excluded because SKF's own AR does not list it among its competitive set — RBC's aerospace-defence mix puts it closer to industrial-specialty peers (Heico, TransDigm) than to industrial-bearings peers.

The resulting six-name universe (SKF + 5 peers) gives **two European, three Japanese, one North American** observations — heavy on European/Japanese which reflects the actual structure of the global bearings industry rather than analyst-convenience geographic balance.

## 4. Profitability Benchmarking

|------|--------------------------:|--------------------------:|------------------:|-----------:|-----------:|
| Timken | 15.9% | 16.2% | 9.5% | 14.1% | 6.9% |
| **AB SKF (FY2024)** | **10.5% reported** | **12.3% adjusted** | **7.0% reported** | **11.7%** | **6.1%** |
| **AB SKF (FY2025)** | **8.5% reported (AR)** | **12.7% adjusted (AR)** | **4.6% reported** | **7.4% (AR rolling-avg)** | **4.29%** |
| Schaeffler AG | 4.7% (combined) | 6.4% | 1.5% | 3.2% | 1.3% |
| NSK | 4.5% | 5.1% | 2.5% | 3.7% | 1.9% |
| JTEKT | 3.6% | 4.2% | 1.8% | 3.1% | 1.5% |
| NTN Corporation | 3.4% | 3.8% | 1.4% | 2.7% | 1.2% |

**Three observations stand out.**

**First — the bearings industry is bifurcated.** Timken operates at *roughly 4x the reported operating margin* of the European/Japanese cohort (Schaeffler, NSK, NTN, JTEKT). This is not a single-year cyclical artefact: looking back across the FY2020–FY2024 window, the gap is structural. Two explanations dominate the industry literature: (i) Timken runs a higher-mix product portfolio (aerospace, mining, energy, oil and gas, plus the Power Transmission segment) with stronger pricing power and weaker exposure to automotive tier-1 pricing pressure; (ii) Timken completed its structural rightsizing cycle earlier (the major Timken steel-business spin-off and bearings refocusing was 2014; the Spinea acquisition and Industrial Motion build-out 2018–2021).

**Second — SKF on the reported basis sits in the middle of the bifurcation**, above the European/Japanese cohort but well below Timken. On the *adjusted* basis SKF publishes, SKF moves clearly above Schaeffler, NSK, NTN, and JTEKT but remains below Timken and short of SKF's own 14% adjusted-operating-margin long-term target. This is the most analytically important slide in this benchmark for the SKF management team: **adjusted profitability lifts SKF from "middle of the pack" to "second only to Timken" within the peer set.**

**Third — SKF's FY2025 reading is a setback inside an otherwise rising trajectory.** Reported operating margin fell from 10.5% FY2024 to 8.5% FY2025 (per AR 2025 Seven-Year Review), but the adjusted margin actually *expanded* from 12.3% to 12.7%. The peer set provides no precedent for confusing this signal: Schaeffler's adjusted margin contracted to 6.4% in 2024 (worse than SKF in either direction); the Japanese trio shows structurally low margins with little year-on-year movement; NTN in particular has been in multi-year restructuring with persistent low-single-digit margins. SKF's FY2025 cycle reading on the adjusted basis is the second-strongest in the peer set after Timken, despite the worst reported optics among the bearings majors.

**ROE comparison reinforces the same narrative.** SKF's FY2024 ROE of 11.7% places it second in the peer set, narrowly behind Timken (14.1%) and well ahead of Schaeffler (3.2%), NSK (3.7%), JTEKT (3.1%), and NTN (2.7%). The FY2025 compression to **7.4% (per AR 2025 rolling-average convention)** or 6.86% on a start-of-year basis drops SKF to *second-from-top* — below Timken, but still well above the European/Japanese quartet. The peer-relative reading is meaningfully more flattering than the absolute reading.

## 5. Capital Structure & Liquidity Benchmarking

| Firm | Total Debt Ratio | LT Debt/Equity | TIE (interest coverage) | Current Ratio | Net Debt/EBITDA |
|------|----:|----:|----:|----:|----:|
| **AB SKF (FY2025)** | **47.7%** | **25.5%** | **5.64x** | **2.11x** | **1.0x (AR)** |
| Timken | 52.3% | 56.8% | 6.2x | 2.46x | 1.9x |
| NSK | 49.8% | 38.2% | 7.8x | 1.95x | 1.1x |
| NTN Corporation | 58.5% | 65.0% | 5.0x | 1.55x | 2.0x |
| JTEKT | 56.4% | 42.7% | 5.5x | 1.62x | 1.6x |
| Schaeffler AG | 67.2% (post-Vitesco) | 89.4% | 2.9x | 1.34x | 3.4x |

**Three reads:**

**SKF is the least-levered firm in the peer set on total debt ratio (47.7%)** and tied for the lowest leverage on net debt / EBITDA (1.0x, per AR Seven-Year Review). The Japanese peers carry mid-tier leverage (50–58% debt ratios); Timken carries 52% reflecting both larger acquisition programs and lower equity-financing costs in the US market; NTN sits at the high end of the Japanese cohort (58.5%) consistent with its multi-year restructuring. Schaeffler stands out as the most-levered (67% post-Vitesco merger), which constrains its capital-allocation optionality more than any peer.

**Interest coverage tells a similar story.** SKF's TIE of 5.64x is comfortably above the >4x healthy threshold and second only to NSK (7.8x). NTN's TIE of 5.0x is mid-pack. Schaeffler's TIE of 2.9x is the only reading I would describe as *concerning* in the peer set — combined with its 67% debt ratio, Schaeffler has materially less room to absorb a multi-quarter demand shock.

**Liquidity is broadly adequate across the peer set**, with current ratios above 1.5x for every firm except Schaeffler. SKF's 2.11x sits at the upper end (above all four Japanese peers and slightly below Timken's 2.46x). The bearings industry as a whole is not capital-distressed on the working capital cycle — even Schaeffler, the most stretched name, prints a 1.34x current ratio that does not flag liquidity stress.

**Strategic read on capital structure**: SKF has the most conservative balance sheet in the European bearings space and one of the most conservative in the global peer set. This translates to **optionality** — for buybacks, for accretive M&A (recall the targeted EUR 1B+ Connected Technologies platform investment in SKF's strategy), or for dividend resilience through cyclical troughs and the planned 2026 Automotive separation. The peer benchmark **confirms** that capital structure is not the bottleneck on returns — exactly the point made in §2d of the Final Analysis. The lever is operating margin, not financial leverage.

## 6. Efficiency & Working Capital Benchmarking

| Firm | Asset Turnover | Inventory Turnover | Days in Inventory | Days Sales Outstanding | Cash Conversion Cycle (est.) |
|------|--------------:|-------------------:|------------------:|----------------------:|------------------------------:|
| Timken | 0.88x | 3.85x | 95 days | 58 days | ~115 days |
| **AB SKF (FY2025)** | **0.77x (0.82x per AR)** | **2.38x** | **153 days (lagged) / 138 days (forward run-rate)** | **65 days (per AR comments)** | **~180 days lagged / 165 forward** |
| NSK | 0.74x | 2.55x | 143 days | 64 days | ~175 days |
| JTEKT | 0.69x | 3.10x | 118 days | 71 days | ~155 days |
| Schaeffler AG | 0.71x | 2.85x | 128 days | 55 days | ~155 days |
| NTN Corporation | 0.65x | 2.45x | 149 days | 75 days | ~180 days |

**Three reads:**

**SKF's asset turnover (0.77x) is roughly at the bearings-peer median**, just behind Timken (0.88x) and ahead of NSK (0.74x), JTEKT (0.69x), and Schaeffler (0.71x). Asset turnover in the 0.65–0.90x range appears to be the structural band for bearings manufacturers; any improvement would come from product-mix shift toward less-asset-intensive segments (services, condition monitoring, software) rather than core bearings throughput.

**Inventory efficiency is where SKF's most actionable peer gap appears.** Days in inventory at 153 (lagged) compares unfavourably to Timken's 95 days — a *60-day gap* representing roughly MSEK 10B of working capital that Timken does not need to carry. On a more apples-to-apples comparison, SKF's forward run-rate of 138 days (using current-period inventory rather than start-of-year) brings the gap to a still-material 43 days versus Timken or 10 days versus JTEKT. The peer benchmark **strengthens** Recommendation 2 in the Final Analysis: the inventory release target should be peer-informed, not arbitrary.

**Receivables management is consistent with industrial-B2B norms** across all peers — DSO in the 50–70 day range. SKF's 66 days is unexceptional; not a concern, not a strength.

**Cash conversion cycle** (estimated as days inventory + DSO − days payable, with peer-level payable estimates rough) places SKF at ~180 days lagged or ~165 days on the forward run-rate. This is mid-pack but again driven entirely by the inventory line — SKF's other working capital components are competitive.

**Strategic read**: Efficiency benchmarking surfaces inventory as the one peer-relative weakness where the gap is both quantified and actionable. If SKF can land 120 days inventory (closer to JTEKT) over FY2026–27, the MSEK 3,151 working capital release identified in Final Analysis Recommendation 2 is the achievable interim target — and Timken's 95-day stretch benchmark implies a total of **MSEK 7,427 of release potential at full peer parity** (of which MSEK 4,276 is the incremental release moving from a 120-day interim to a 95-day stretch target over the longer horizon). Whether the 95-day Timken-parity stretch is achievable inside the SKF product mix (long-tail SKUs, distributor network) is a more difficult question that this benchmark cannot answer directly, but the lever is real.

| NSK | 18.2x | 7.8x | 0.65x | 3.2% | (5%) |

| NTN Corporation | 11.0x | 5.5x | 0.55x | 2.8% | (15%) |

**Three reads:**

**Timken trades at the multiple ceiling** of the bearings-industry comparator set — 14.8x P/E and 9.5x EV/EBITDA — reflecting its structurally higher margins and earlier-completed rightsizing. This is informative for SKF in that it sets the realistic upside multiple SKF could earn if FY2026–27 margin recovery is executed and the Automotive separation closes cleanly: roughly 14–16x P/E on normalised earnings.

**SKF's FY2025 P/E of 26x is elevated** but reflects the trough-earnings effect — the 39% EPS decline (8.62 SEK FY2025 vs 14.22 SEK FY2024 per AR) drives the denominator down. On a normalised earnings basis (using FY2024 EPS 14.22 or a midcycle estimate ~SEK 13–15), SKF's forward P/E reads closer to ~17x, which is in the upper half of the European/Japanese bearings band (Schaeffler 14.6x, NSK 18.2x, JTEKT 12.5x, NTN 11.0x) and above Timken's 14.8x cyclical multiple. The market is **pricing the post-rightsizing recovery plus the 2026 Automotive separation optionality into the multiple** — this is consistent with the MSEK 56,257 MVA reading and the 2.01x P/B in the Final Analysis.

**EV/EBITDA at ~8.2x** for SKF places it above the European/Japanese peer median (~6.5x median across Schaeffler 7.2x, NSK 7.8x, NTN 5.5x, JTEKT 5.8x) and below Timken (9.5x). This is the multiple I would lean on most heavily in CFO communication — it strips out the trough-earnings noise that distorts P/E and the merger-accounting effects that distort Schaeffler. SKF trades at a roughly 25% EV/EBITDA premium to the Japanese cohort, consistent with the market crediting both the restructuring story and the higher-margin Industrial-only post-separation outlook.

**P/B of 2.01x** is comparable to Timken (2.0x — essentially tied) and meaningfully above the Japanese cohort (0.55–0.65x). The Japanese P/B compression is well-documented (TSE prime market structural issue, not bearings-specific) and should be discounted in the bearings-industry read. Within the meaningful comparator set, SKF's P/B premium is consistent with above-peer ROE on the adjusted basis.

**Dividend yield** sits SKF at ~3.5% (FY2025 SEK 7.75 proposed dividend on the ~SEK 245.80 B-share close) — comparable to the Japanese peers (NSK 3.2%, JTEKT 3.6%, NTN 2.8%) and below Schaeffler's stressed 4.8% yield. This places SKF at a *normal* yield for the peer group and supports the analysis in Final Analysis Recommendation 3 that the dividend is sustainable through one weak year but warrants explicit conditional framing.

## 8. Cross-Cutting Findings & Strategic Implications for SKF

Five findings emerge when the per-category readings are integrated.

**Finding 1 — The bearings industry is bifurcated by geography and segment mix, not just by cycle.** The European/Japanese cohort (Schaeffler, NSK, NTN, JTEKT) operates at structurally lower reported operating margins (3–5% pre-tax) than Timken (16% pre-tax) by roughly a factor of four. This gap has held across multiple cycles and reflects mix differences (Power Transmission + aerospace/defence exposure at Timken; auto-tier-1 pricing pressure in Europe/Japan) and Timken's earlier-completed rightsizing programs. SKF's adjusted-margin trajectory at 12.7% places it **above** the European/Japanese cohort and approaches but does not match Timken's 16% — *the strategic transformation SKF management has been describing is, at the adjusted-margin level, working*, with the planned 2026 Automotive separation expected to lift the Industrial-only run-rate closer to Timken's level.

**Finding 2 — Capital structure is not the differentiator.** All six peers operate at leverage within a manageable band (total debt ratio 47–67%, TIE 2.9–7.8x). SKF carries the most conservative leverage in the European cohort and one of the most conservative globally. This *removes* capital structure from the list of strategic levers — there is no peer-credible argument that SKF needs to deleverage further, and there is a defensible argument that SKF could absorb more debt for accretive M&A or buyback without exceeding peer norms. The constraint on increasing leverage is the credit-rating preservation (single-A target), not peer comparison.

**Finding 3 — Inventory is SKF's one structural efficiency gap.** Days in inventory at 153 (lagged) or 138 (forward run-rate) compares unfavourably to Timken (95 days) and JTEKT (118 days). Working backwards from a 120-day peer-realistic target, the MSEK 3.1B working capital release identified in Final Analysis Recommendation 2 is the achievable interim target — a Timken-parity 95-day stretch target would imply **MSEK 7.4B of total release** (the additional MSEK 4.3B coming from the 120→95-day longer-horizon stretch).

**Finding 4 — SKF is mid-pack on size but second-quartile on adjusted profitability.** The combination of size median, geographic diversification leader (within the European set), and second-quartile adjusted-margin profitability is a defensible *peer-relative* position. The disconnect between this peer-relative position and the FY2025 reported earnings shock is what the investor communication has to bridge — exactly the point of Final Analysis Recommendation 4.

**Finding 5 — Schaeffler and NTN are the cautionary peers**, not SKF. Schaeffler's combined-entity FY2024 reading (4.7% reported operating margin, 3.2% ROE, 67% total debt ratio, 2.9x TIE) and NTN's multi-year restructuring track record (3.4% margin, 2.7% ROE, 58% debt ratio) are both materially weaker than SKF on every dimension *except* revenue scale (Schaeffler bought scale via the Vitesco merger; NTN's smaller size reflects its narrower scope). The Schaeffler and NTN comparisons should appear in SKF investor materials — not adversarially, but to demonstrate that the European/Japanese bearings industry has materially different operating-stress profiles within a single peer band.

**Strategic implications for SKF's FY2026 communication**:

1. **Anchor the peer comparison to the adjusted-margin basis** — this places SKF firmly in the second-of-six position (above Schaeffler, NSK, NTN, JTEKT) and meaningfully closer to Timken. The reported-basis comparison is misleading on cycle effects.

2. **Use Timken as the aspirational margin benchmark.** Timken's 15.9% operating margin reflects bearings + power transmission integration and broader industrial-end-market mix that SKF has analogues for (Connected Technologies platform, condition monitoring services, planned post-Automotive-separation Industrial focus).

3. **Quantify the working capital release using the peer benchmark** — MSEK 3.1B over FY2026–27 at 120-day interim parity is defensible without making a heroic operational claim; the MSEK 7.4B at 95-day Timken-parity stretch is the longer-horizon ceiling.

4. **Schaeffler is the right caution; the Japanese cohort is the right reminder.** The European/Japanese band is where SKF would land *without* successful margin expansion. Avoiding that band by FY2027 is the strategic stake.

The peer benchmark does not change the Stage 5 Final Analysis conclusion — it *anchors* it in industry-relative terms. SKF's path to FY2026 reported operating margin ≥8.5% (the KPI commitment proposed in Final Analysis §9) would place it above the entire European/Japanese cohort on reported figures alone, before any adjusted-figure normalisation. That is a peer-relative outcome the board can defensibly commit to externally.

## 9. Methodology Notes & Data Limitations

**SKF figures** (FY2024 and FY2025) are verified end-to-end against the Stage 3 Excel model at [`models/builds/2026-05-19-dang-skf-financials.xlsx`](../models/builds/2026-05-19-dang-skf-financials.xlsx) and SKF's FY2025 Annual Report; the verification table at [`analysis/validation/2026-05-27-dang-skf-stage5-verification.md`](../analysis/validation/2026-05-27-dang-skf-stage5-verification.md) cross-checks nine ratios by hand.

**Peer figures** (Schaeffler, NSK, NTN, JTEKT, Timken) are **approximate FY2024 reported values** drawn from publicly available company financial summaries (annual-report press releases, investor factsheets, consensus industry databases). They have **not been independently traced line-by-line to each company's audited annual report** within the scope of this Stage 5 deliverable. Figures are rounded to one decimal place; the second decimal should be read as approximate. A reader who needs verified peer precision should consult the underlying annual reports (links in each peer's investor-relations page) and treat this document as a positioning framework rather than a precision-verified data table.

**Peer selection basis**: The five peers (Schaeffler, NSK, NTN, JTEKT, Timken) are the exact bearings-industry competitors SKF itself identifies in the Annual Report 2025 (page 5): "the market is led by SKF, along with other major international players like Schaeffler, Timken, NSK, NTN and JTEKT.
